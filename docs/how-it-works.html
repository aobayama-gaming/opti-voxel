How opti-voxel works

The whole engine on one page: which thread does what, in what order, with which loops, how many times, and where the time goes. Read from src/ by hand and checked against the traced forest fly (forest5cm, error 2, run 20261024-151347). Every step names the function to open.

1 · The picture

Six threads take part. The camera moving makes the discoverer decide which chunks should exist. The main thread turns the difference into a queue and sends batches of up to 128 chunks to the render thread, which records the GPU work. The GPU computes the terrain field (the cascade), meshes it and writes quads straight into pages that Godot draws. The CPU never sees the geometry; it only gets back a small "where did each chunk land" list about 80 ms later. Trees and rocks are computed beside all this on the worker pool, and a chunk waits until its trees are ready.

discoverer thread main thread worker pool (+ FieldGPU thread) render thread GPU

Three words used everywhere

Chunk. A cube of 28³ voxels plus a 2-voxel skirt each side: the mesher samples 32³ points per chunk. A chunk of LOD L has voxels 2L times bigger (LOD 0 is 5 cm here, LOD 14 is ~820 m). A chunk id is an integer position plus its LOD. chunk_math.hpp:12

Scan. The set of chunks that should be shown from the current camera: near chunks fine, far chunks coarse, nothing far from the surface. ~35,000 chunks, of which ~10,000 have any surface. chunk_discoverer.cpp:355

Page. A Godot MultiMesh plus a texture that the GPU fills with quads directly. A batch takes 16 fresh pages. Chunks are packed into them, and half-empty pages are compacted later. ~750 pages live at the end of the run. quad_page_pool.hpp:16

Cascade. How the terrain value is computed on the GPU: start from a coarse grid, then refine it level by level (each level twice as fine), adding the finer noise bands at each level, down to the chunk's own 32³ points. cascade.hpp:13

2 · A scan: which chunks should exist

The discoverer thread sleeps until the camera enters a new chunk (or turns past a remembered view), then walks an octree from the coarsest LOD down. It's a breadth-first walk: all nodes of one LOD form one "wave", and the wave's survivors split into the next wave.

4 · One mesh batch on the render thread and GPU

This is where "how many times does the cascade run per chunk" is answered. The GPU doesn't loop over chunks: every dispatch covers all the chunks of the batch at once (the chunk is the dispatch's third dimension). So per batch, each cascade level is one dispatch and each mesh pass is one dispatch. Every chunk goes through every level exactly once.

5 · Trees and rocks: the instance stream

Trees and rocks aren't meshes. They are SDF records that the cascade's level 0 folds into the ground, so a chunk can only be meshed once it knows every record that reaches it. Those records are computed on demand, in pieces called keys. Each key is a pure function of its position, so the result is the same whatever order they stream in. instance_stream.hpp:18, scatter_source.hpp:10

Worker pooldrain tasks of InstanceStream at low priority · instance_stream.cpp:407
  • a chunk's admit lists the keys it needs and asks for the missing ones; their jobs are ranked by the chunk's queue (in view and fine first)instance_stream.cpp:86
  • while there are jobs: take the best-ranked one, run it, land it (which may start its dependents right away)job_expand 3.4 · content_blobs 2.0 · content_parts 1 per frame
    • site tile (walk): scan the ground cell by cell to find where trees stand. The ground readings are batched and sent to the FieldGPU thread, which evaluates them on its own device and answers in ~1.5 ms.→ FieldGPUfield_gpu.cpp:61
    • part tile (LOD L): grow the trees of the tile into records (trunk, branches, leaves as SDF shapes) at that LOD
    • blob tile (far LODs): summarise many trees per cell as a few blobs (moment layers), so distant forest costs the same at any tree countmoment_layers.cpp
    • land: the key's records go into slots of the GPU table; parked chunks whose last key landed go back to the front of the mesher's queueinstance_stream.cpp:472

During load, the pool spends ~18 ms per frame waiting for FieldGPU answers in site walks. Once the forest around the camera is resident, the stream is nearly idle in the fly (2% busy).

The "stays drawn until replaced" rule is why there are no holes while flying. A coarse chunk that splits into 8 finer ones keeps showing until they've landed, and coverage hides the parts of it that the landed ones already cover. chunk_memory.hpp:25

7 · Where the GPU frame goes (fly)

The fly is GPU-bound: the main thread works ~3 ms of a ~22 ms frame, while the GPU is busy 99% of the time (27.3 ms of GPU work per captured frame). Meshing and drawing share it about half and half, and one dispatch dominates the meshing half.

What this says for optimisation: in the fly, the level-0 dispatch (finest noise + folding the trees into each sample) costs about as much as all of Godot's drawing. Load shows the same: 17.2 of 33 ms. Shadows (6 ms) come next. Everything on the CPU, the scan included, runs on other threads and doesn't hold the frame.

Ground or trees? The mesher times each batch's cascade and bins it by the batch's list length (gpu_by_list_words in stats.json). Batches with no tree in their lists run the whole cascade in 1.06 ms, against 5.13 ms on average in the fly. So the tree fold is ~79% of the cascade and ~66% of all meshing GPU; the ground's finest bands are cheap. The cost follows the list: 2.1 ms at ~90 records per batch, 5.7 ms at ~780, 10.5 ms at ~5,600, 23.7 ms at ~22,000. A quarter of the batches (≥2,048 records) carry half of it.

Why it's expensive: in arch_fold (archetype_table.cpp, generated GLSL) every one of the chunk's 32³ samples walks the chunk's whole list, reading each 160-byte record to test its box. Workgroups are 64 samples in a row, so they can't share that test. Levers: cull the list per brick of samples (4³ workgroups, the list tested once into shared memory), test boxes from a compact array before touching the full record, and fewer or lighter records at coarse LODs (per-source LOD rules, blobs sooner).
